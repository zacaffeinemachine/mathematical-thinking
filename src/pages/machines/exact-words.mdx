<ThemeNote>
A password machine has an easy shape and one honest difficulty.

The shape is a corridor. One circle per letter matched so far, one arrow
along the corridor for the right letter, and every wrong letter falling out
of the corridor into **dead**. The end of the corridor is the accepting
circle, and even the correct word followed by one more letter has to leave
it, because the machine accepts that word and nothing longer.

The difficulty is the alphabet. Twenty-six letters means twenty-six arrows
out of every circle, which nobody wants to draw. The convention used on the
first page is the fix: draw the arrows you care about, then one arrow
labelled `x` meaning **any other letter**. Say once, in words, that this is
what `x` means, and the diagram stays honest.

When two words are wanted, the corridors share whatever prefix the words
share and split where the words split.
</ThemeNote>

<Problem n={1} title="One short word">
Accept `hat` and nothing else.

</Problem>

<Problem n={2} title="One longer word">
Accept `code` and nothing else.
<Tests accept={["code"]} reject={["", "cod", "codes", "coda", "ccode", "node"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={3} title="Two words that share nothing">
Accept `hate` and `qrmt`, and nothing else.

The two words have no first letter in common, so the corridors part company
at once. How many circles in total, and how many of them are accepting?
<Tests accept={["hate", "qrmt"]} reject={["", "hat", "qrm", "hateqrmt", "haterqrmt", "hqartme"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={4} title="Three words, two of which start alike">
Accept `start`, `stop` and `pause`, and nothing else.

`start` and `stop` share their first two letters, so their corridors should
share circles until they part. Do not draw two separate corridors and hope
nobody notices.
<Tests accept={["start", "stop", "pause"]} reject={["", "st", "sto", "starts", "stopp", "paus", "startstop"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={5} title="Words that are prefixes of each other">
Accept `to`, `too` and `tool`, and nothing else.

Here one word is the beginning of the next. The corridor does not branch at
all, which means some circles along it are accepting and some are not.
<Tests accept={["to", "too", "tool"]} reject={["", "t", "tools", "toll", "tooll", "ot"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={6} title="Every short word">
Accept every string of length 2 or less, over the 26 letters, and nothing
longer.

This one is not a corridor. Notice how few circles it needs even though the
number of accepted words is 1 + 26 + 676.
<Tests accept={["", "a", "z", "hi", "qq"]} reject={["cat", "hate", "abcd", "aaa"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={7} title="A word at the start, anything after" hard>
Accept exactly the strings that begin with `un`.

Compare this with problem 2. There, the accepting circle had to be left
again on the next letter. Here it must not be. Say what changed.
<Tests accept={["un", "und", "undo", "unhappy", "unn"]} reject={["", "u", "n", "nu", "sun", "aun"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={8} title="A word repeated" hard>
The alphabet here is just `{a, b}`. Accept exactly the strings made by
writing `ab` some number of times over: the empty string, `ab`, `abab`,
`ababab`, and so on.
<Tests accept={["", "ab", "abab", "ababab"]} reject={["a", "b", "ba", "aab", "aba", "abb"]} />
</Problem>

<Problem n={9} title="No letter twice in a row" hard>
The alphabet here is `{a, b}`. Accept exactly the non-empty strings in which
no two neighbouring letters are the same.

The empty string is **not** accepted, which costs a circle. Say which one
and why.
<Tests accept={["a", "b", "ab", "ba", "abab", "babab"]} reject={["", "aa", "bb", "ababb", "abba"]} />
</Problem>
