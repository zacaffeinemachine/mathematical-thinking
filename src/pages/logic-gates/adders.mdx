On this page a number is set on a row of switches, and each switch is worth a
fixed amount. The switch $a_0$ is worth 1, the switch $a_1$ is worth 2, the
switch $a_2$ is worth 4, and each switch is worth double the one before it. The
number is the total of the switches that are up. A number set on $n$ switches
in this way is an **$n$-bit number**. So a 1-bit number is 0 or 1, a 2-bit
number is one of 0 to 3, and a 3-bit number is one of 0 to 7.

Two numbers are added the way you add on paper. One number sits on the row
$a$, the other on the row $b$ below it, and the sum appears on the row of
bulbs $s$ at the bottom, worth 1, 2, 4, 8 in the same way. The switches $a_0$
and $b_0$ and the bulb $s_0$ share a column, and so do $a_1$, $b_1$ and $s_1$,
and so on.

<CircuitProblem n={4} title="One bit more" hard>
You are given a sealed box that adds two $n$-bit numbers and shows the sum on
${n + 1}$ wires. Using one such box and as many gates as you like, build a
circuit that adds two ${(n + 1)}$-bit numbers.

Try it first for $n = 2$. The button *2-bit adder* above the canvas places a
box that adds two 2-bit numbers. It adds the number on its pins $x_1$ and $x_0$
to the number on its pins $y_1$ and $y_0$, and shows the sum on its pins $z_2$,
$z_1$ and $z_0$. Here $x_0$, $y_0$ and $z_0$ are worth 1, $x_1$, $y_1$ and
$z_1$ are worth 2, and $z_2$ is worth 4. Use the box to add the number on $a_2$,
$a_1$ and $a_0$ to the number on $b_2$, $b_1$ and $b_0$, and show the sum on the
bulbs $s_3$, $s_2$, $s_1$ and $s_0$.
