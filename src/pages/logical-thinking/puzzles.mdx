## 1. The Hat Hurdle

Four perfect logicians (named **1**, **2**, **3**, and **4**) have been
captured by a warden. To win their freedom, they must solve a puzzle. The
warden places them in a line as shown in the diagram.

- **Logician 1** is at the highest position and can see the hats of Logician
  2 and Logician 3.
- **Logician 2** is in the middle and can see the hat of Logician 3.
- **Logician 3** is in front of Logician 2 and can see only a brick wall.
- **Logician 4** is on the other side of the brick wall, unable to see
  anyone, and no one can see them.

The warden places a hat on each logician's head from a set of **two red
hats** and **two blue hats**. No logician can see the colour of their own
hat.

  />
</figure>

Here is the important fine print.

1. **Perfect logic.** All four are perfect logicians. They will make any
   deduction that is logically possible, and each knows that the other three
   are equally perfect.
2. **Shared knowledge.** Every logician knows the entire setup, meaning the
   positions, the lines of sight, and that there are exactly two red and two
   blue hats in total.
3. **No communication.** No speaking, no gestures. The logicians answer the
   warden's questions and nothing more.
4. **Certainty required.** A logician will state their hat colour only when
   they are 100% certain. They will never guess.

The warden then conducts the following round of questioning, and every
logician hears every question and every answer.

1. The warden asks **Logician 4**: *Do you know the colour of your hat?*
   &nbsp; Logician 4 replies, *"I do not know."*
2. The warden asks **Logician 3**: *Do you know the colour of your hat?*
   &nbsp; Logician 3 replies, *"I do not know."*
3. The warden asks **Logician 2**: *Do you know the colour of your hat?*
   &nbsp; Logician 2 replies, *"I do not know."*
4. The warden asks **Logician 1**, the one with the best view: *Do you know
   the colour of your hat?* &nbsp; Logician 1 also replies, *"I do not
   know."*

And then, out of the silence, **Logician 2** speaks up and correctly
announces their hat colour.

**Suppose that Logician 3 is wearing a red hat.** Fill in what is now
settled, and mark as undetermined whatever is not.

Three perfect logicians, **Elara**, **Phoebe**, and **Orion**, were given a
puzzle. A hat, coloured either red or blue, was placed on each of their
heads. They could each see the hats of the other two, but not their own.

After a moment of looking at one another, they all spoke at the same time:

> *"I see at least one red hat!"*

A silence then fell as they thought about this statement. Then, all at once,
they spoke again:

> *"I don't know what colour my hat is."*

After a little more time passed, a realisation struck all three. Together,
they exclaimed:

> *"Now I know my hat colour!"*

**What colour is each hat?**

## 3. One Answer, Three Ways

A ruby lies in the **Coral** box or the **Amber** box, and Thalia knows which.

- **a)** You ask Thalia, *"Is the ruby in the Coral box?"*, and she answers
  yes.
- **b)** You ask Thalia, *"Is the answer to 'Is the ruby in the Coral box?'
  yes?"*, and she answers yes.
- **c)** Ariadne, of whom you know nothing at all, says: *"If Thalia were
  asked whether the ruby is in the Coral box, she would answer yes."*

The three are separate situations, not three things that happen one after
another. In each, **where is the ruby?**
