## 1. The Hat Hurdle

Four perfect logicians (named **1**, **2**, **3**, and **4**) have been
captured by a warden. To win their freedom, they must solve a puzzle. The
warden places them in a line as shown in the diagram.

- **Logician 1** is at the highest position and can see the hats of Logician
  2 and Logician 3.
- **Logician 2** is in the middle and can see the hat of Logician 3.
- **Logician 3** is in front of Logician 2 and can see only a brick wall.
- **Logician 4** is on the other side of the brick wall, unable to see
  anyone, and no one can see them.

The warden places a hat on each logician's head from a set of **two red
hats** and **two blue hats**. No logician can see the colour of their own
hat.

Here is the important fine print.

1. **Perfect logic.** All four are perfect logicians. They will make any
   deduction that is logically possible, and each knows that the other three
   are equally perfect.
2. **Shared knowledge.** Every logician knows the entire setup, meaning the
   positions, the lines of sight, and that there are exactly two red and two
   blue hats in total.
3. **No communication.** No speaking, no gestures. The logicians answer the
   warden's questions and nothing more.
4. **Certainty required.** A logician will state their hat colour only when
   they are 100% certain. They will never guess.

The warden then conducts the following round of questioning, and every
logician hears every question and every answer.

1. The warden asks **Logician 4**: *Do you know the colour of your hat?*
   &nbsp; Logician 4 replies, *"I do not know."*
2. The warden asks **Logician 3**: *Do you know the colour of your hat?*
   &nbsp; Logician 3 replies, *"I do not know."*
3. The warden asks **Logician 2**: *Do you know the colour of your hat?*
   &nbsp; Logician 2 replies, *"I do not know."*
4. The warden asks **Logician 1**, the one with the best view: *Do you know
   the colour of your hat?* &nbsp; Logician 1 also replies, *"I do not
   know."*

And then, out of the silence, **Logician 2** speaks up and correctly
announces their hat colour.

**Suppose that Logician 3 is wearing a red hat.** Fill in what is now
settled, and mark as undetermined whatever is not.

Three perfect logicians, **Elara**, **Phoebe**, and **Orion**, were given a
puzzle. A hat, coloured either red or blue, was placed on each of their
heads. They could each see the hats of the other two, but not their own.

After a moment of looking at one another, they all spoke at the same time:

> *"I see at least one red hat!"*

A silence then fell as they thought about this statement. Then, all at once,
they spoke again:

> *"I don't know what colour my hat is."*

After a little more time passed, a realisation struck all three. Together,
they exclaimed:

> *"Now I know my hat colour!"*

**What colour is each hat?**

## 3. Four Hats in a Chest

In a chest there are four hats: two are **red** and two are **blue**.

Three wise sages, **Elara**, **Phoebe** and **Orion**, are gathered for a
test of logic. Each sage is blindfolded and places one of the four hats upon
their head. They do not see the colour of the hat they have chosen. One hat
remains in the chest, and its colour is unknown to them. The sages'
blindfolds are then removed.

The rules are as follows.

- Each sage can see the hats worn by the other two sages.
- No sage can see the hat on their own head.
- No sage can see the leftover hat inside the chest.

Each sage reasons perfectly, and each knows that the other two do.

The sages look at one another. Elara makes the following silent observation:

> *Phoebe is wearing a blue hat, and Orion is wearing a red hat.*

That is what Elara sees, and it is all we are told. The colour of Elara's own
hat is not given, to us or to her.

**How many of the three sages can name the colour of their own hat with
certainty, and who is it?**

## 4. Three Sages, Five Hats

Three logicians, **Ariadne**, **Selene** and **Thalia**, are each wearing a
hat. They can see the colours of the others' hats but not their own. They
know that their hats were drawn from a group of five hats: **three red** and
**two blue**. The two hats left over are put away unseen.

Each logician reasons perfectly, and each knows that the other two do. A
question is posed to each logician in turn, and every logician hears the
answers given before her own.

1. Ariadne is asked what colour her hat is. She responds, *"I do not know."*
2. Selene is asked what colour her hat is. She also says, *"I do not know."*
3. When Thalia is asked what colour her hat is, she answers correctly.

**What colour is Thalia's hat, and what told her?**

## 5. Olympian Cards

High atop the peaks of Mount Olympus, a game of pure logic unfolds.

**Zeus**, the King of Gods, has fashioned a deck of six celestial cards, each
inscribed with a different one of the numbers 1 through 6. He summons
**Athena**, the Goddess of Wisdom, and **Apollo**, the God of Truth. He deals
one secret card to Athena and one secret card to Apollo, and keeps the other
four. Each deity sees only their own card. Athena and Apollo each reason
perfectly, and each knows that the other does.

Zeus then proclaims to them both:

> *"The numbers you hold sum up to either 5 or 7."*

A silence falls, followed by this exchange.

- **Athena speaks:** *"Father, despite my wisdom, I do not know whether our
  sum is 5 or 7."*
- **Apollo speaks:** *"Hearing this, sister, I too must confess: I do not
  know whether our sum is 5 or 7."*

**Hermes**, the Messenger God, has witnessed the whole exchange from the
shadows. He is a perfect logician himself, and he begins to deduce the truth.

**Which card numbers can Hermes be certain that Athena does not hold, and
which can he be certain that Apollo does not hold?**
